NIRNAY-CSR: Complete Production-Grade System Specification

Document purpose: This specification defines the product, architecture, data model, AI components, workflows, security controls, APIs, deployment model, evaluation framework and implementation sequence required to build NIRNAY-CSR as a fully deployable system.

This document is intended to be sufficiently explicit for an autonomous software-development agent, engineering team or coding system to design, implement, test and deploy the platform without having to infer the product's fundamental behaviour.

1. System Identity

1.1 Product Name

NIRNAY-CSR

1.2 Full Product Description

Autonomous CSR Opportunity Intelligence and Decision Assurance System

1.3 Primary Purpose

NIRNAY-CSR helps Indian NGOs and corporate CSR teams determine whether a funding opportunity, corporate programme, historical CSR record or public scheme is relevant, currently actionable, legally or operationally eligible, sufficiently supported by evidence and safe to pursue.

1.4 Core Product Statement

NIRNAY-CSR continuously ingests permitted public information and organization-supplied compliance documents, classifies the true state of every opportunity, rejects ineligible routes, detects contradictions across evidence sources and generates an audit-ready decision docket explaining the decision and required next action.

1.5 Product Tagline

From plausible opportunities to evidence-backed decisions.

1.6 Primary Domain

Sustainability and Green Technology, with AI used as an enabling technology.

2. Problem Definition

CSR-related information exists across open-government datasets, company websites, annual reports, CSR policies, RFP documents, NGO credentials, government portals and private marketplaces.

These records may contain similar words while representing entirely different situations:

  • A historical CSR expenditure record
  • A completed project
  • A Board-approved project
  • A live request for proposal
  • An open application channel
  • A citizen-benefit scheme
  • A private directory entry
  • An unsupported self-declared claim
  • An expired opportunity
  • An opportunity with unknown current status

Generic search engines and language models may retrieve all of these as semantically relevant. However, relevance does not establish actionability, eligibility or trustworthiness.

2.1 Core Failures Addressed

2.1.1 Historical-as-Live Error

Past CSR expenditure is incorrectly presented as an open funding opportunity.

2.1.2 Eligibility Blindness

A matching project is recommended without checking required entity type, compliance documents, geography, budget, deadline, operational history or beneficiary requirements.

2.1.3 Registration-as-Endorsement Error

A registration or portal listing is incorrectly represented as government approval, performance verification or quality certification.

2.1.4 Stale-Source Error

An expired RFP, outdated policy or old project record is presented without temporal context.

2.1.5 Unsupported Certainty

A language model converts incomplete evidence into a confident recommendation.

2.1.6 Cross-Document Identity Conflict

Names, PAN values, registration numbers, entity types or validity dates do not agree across supplied documents.

2.1.7 Manual Due-Diligence Burden

NGOs and CSR teams manually reconcile multiple sources before deciding whether an opportunity is worth pursuing.

3. Product Objectives

  • Identify public CSR opportunities and corporate alignment signals.
  • Separate historical evidence from current actionable opportunities.
  • Reject impossible or ineligible routes before semantic ranking.
  • Detect contradictions among claims, records and documents.
  • Show exactly which evidence supports or weakens every decision.
  • Never represent missing evidence as verified information.
  • Generate an auditable and portable decision report.
  • Continuously monitor sources and re-evaluate affected decisions.
  • Operate autonomously after initial organization onboarding and source configuration.
  • Remain useful even when a government portal has no public API.

4. Non-Goals and Explicit Boundaries

NIRNAY-CSR must not claim or attempt to provide the following:

  • Legal certification of CSR compliance
  • Guaranteed funding
  • Government endorsement of an NGO
  • Complete real-time coverage of every Indian CSR opportunity
  • Automated access to OTP-protected or CAPTCHA-protected systems
  • Automated access to private tax accounts
  • Verification of unpublished corporate intentions
  • Unauthorized scraping of restricted platforms
  • Automatic validation of credentials through undocumented government endpoints
  • Replacement of a qualified lawyer, chartered accountant, compliance professional or CSR auditor

The system provides evidence-backed decision support, not legal certification.

6. Non-Negotiable System Principles

6.1 Evidence Before Assertion

Every factual output must link to one or more stored evidence records.

6.2 Unknown Is a Valid Output

When evidence is incomplete, the result must be UNKNOWN, NEEDS_INFORMATION or NEEDS_HUMAN_REVIEW.

6.3 LLMs Do Not Decide Eligibility

The LLM may extract, classify, summarize and explain. Final mandatory eligibility decisions must be made by deterministic, versioned rules.

6.4 Historical Data Must Never Be Presented as Live Funding

Historical CSR expenditure may indicate corporate interest. It cannot establish an open funding opportunity.

6.5 Registration Must Not Be Presented as Endorsement

A portal registration or identity record proves only what the source explicitly establishes.

6.6 Missing Evidence Must Not Become Certainty

The absence of contrary evidence does not prove a claim.

6.7 Every Decision Must Be Reproducible

The system must store input versions, rule versions, source versions, model versions and timestamps required to reproduce a decision.

6.8 Every Automated Source Must Be Authorized

Automated ingestion must respect access controls, rate limits, robots policies, licence terms and platform restrictions.

7. Core Innovations

7.1 Dual-Axis Decision Classification

The system separately determines:

  1. What kind of record has been discovered?
  2. Can the current organization act on it?
Source-State Axis
  • LIVE_RFP
  • OPEN_APPLICATION_CHANNEL
  • BOARD_APPROVED_PROJECT
  • HISTORICAL_EXPENDITURE
  • COMPLETED_PROJECT
  • CITIZEN_ONLY_SCHEME
  • PRIVATE_LISTING
  • EXPIRED_OPPORTUNITY
  • UNKNOWN_STATUS
Organization-Decision Axis
  • ELIGIBLE
  • INELIGIBLE
  • CONDITIONALLY_ELIGIBLE
  • NEEDS_INFORMATION
  • NEEDS_HUMAN_REVIEW
  • NOT_APPLICABLE

7.2 Rejection-First Eligibility Engine

Mandatory failures are evaluated before similarity or strategic ranking.

7.3 Cross-Source Contradiction Engine

Claims, entities, credentials, opportunities and sources are connected in an evidence graph and compared for contradictions.

7.4 Audit-Ready Evidence Docket

Every decision includes the source state, eligibility result, triggered rules, supporting evidence, contradictions, evidence strength, timestamps, limitations and next action.

7.5 Continuous Decision Re-Evaluation

When a document expires, RFP changes, company status changes or new evidence is discovered, affected decisions are automatically recalculated.

7.6 Portable Decision-Assurance API

The platform can be used independently or integrated into CSR marketplaces, NGO platforms, corporate systems and internal compliance workflows.

9. Physical Deployment Architecture

9.1 Initial Production Deployment

The initial system must run using Docker Compose on one secured cloud VM.

Recommended VM
  • 8 vCPUs
  • 16 GB RAM
  • 100 GB SSD storage
  • Ubuntu Server LTS
  • Static public IP
  • Domain name with TLS
  • Daily encrypted off-VM backups
Minimum Hackathon VM
  • 4 vCPUs
  • 8 GB RAM
  • 50 GB SSD storage
  • External LLM API

9.2 Containerized Services

  • nginx: TLS termination, reverse proxy and rate limiting
  • frontend: Next.js web application
  • api: FastAPI application
  • worker: asynchronous document and ingestion processing
  • scheduler: recurring jobs and refresh schedules
  • postgres: structured database and pgvector
  • redis: job queue, cache and distributed locks
  • minio: S3-compatible object storage for documents and evidence snapshots
  • clamav: malware scanning for uploaded documents
  • prometheus: metrics collection
  • grafana: operational dashboards
  • loki: central log storage
  • otel-collector: distributed tracing

9.3 Future Scale-Out Deployment

The architecture must allow later migration to Kubernetes or managed services without modifying domain logic.

  • Managed PostgreSQL
  • Managed Redis
  • Object storage such as S3-compatible storage
  • Multiple API replicas
  • Multiple worker pools
  • Separate crawler, document and AI worker queues
  • Autoscaling based on queue depth

14.3 Evidence Completeness

Evidence completeness must be calculated from required evidence fields, not general language-model confidence.


def evidence_completeness(required_fields, resolved_fields):
    required_weight = sum(field.weight for field in required_fields)

    resolved_weight = sum(
        field.weight
        for field in required_fields
        if resolved_fields.get(field.name, {}).get("supported") is True
    )

    if required_weight == 0:
        return 1.0

    return resolved_weight / required_weight

15.1 Required Input Fields

  • Project status language

15.2 Deterministic Classification Rules


def classify_source_state(record):
    if record.explicit_deadline and record.application_route:
        if record.explicit_deadline < today():
            return "EXPIRED_OPPORTUNITY"
        return "LIVE_RFP"

    if record.explicit_open_channel and record.application_route:
        return "OPEN_APPLICATION_CHANNEL"

    if record.board_approved and not record.application_route:
        return "BOARD_APPROVED_PROJECT"

    if record.financial_year and record.expenditure_amount:
        return "HISTORICAL_EXPENDITURE"

    if record.completed_status:
        return "COMPLETED_PROJECT"

    if record.applicant_type == "INDIVIDUAL":
        return "CITIZEN_ONLY_SCHEME"

    if record.source_type == "PRIVATE_DIRECTORY":
        return "PRIVATE_LISTING"

    return "UNKNOWN_STATUS"

15.3 LLM Role

The LLM may extract indicators from unstructured text. It must not issue the final source-state label without deterministic validation.

15.4 Minimum Conditions for LIVE_RFP

  • Identifiable issuer
  • Official or attributable source
  • Explicit application route
  • Current deadline or explicit open status
  • Applicant requirements
  • Publication or update date

If any critical condition is missing, the record must not be labelled as a verified live RFP.

16. Eligibility Rule Engine

16.1 Rule Engine Requirements

  • Deterministic
  • Versioned
  • Configurable
  • Auditable
  • Testable without an LLM
  • Capable of hard failures, warnings and information requests
  • Capable of effective-date versioning
  • Capable of jurisdiction-specific rule packs

16.2 Rule Severity

  • BLOCK: Candidate becomes ineligible.
  • REVIEW: Candidate requires human review.
  • WARNING: Candidate remains eligible with disclosed risk.
  • INFO: Additional context only.

16.3 Example JSON Rule


{
  "rule_code": "CSR1_REQUIRED",
  "version": 1,
  "description": "A valid CSR registration credential is required for this route.",
  "severity": "BLOCK",
  "when": {
    "all": [
      {
        "fact": "opportunity.requirements.csr1_required",
        "operator": "equal",
        "value": true
      },
      {
        "fact": "organization.credentials.csr1.status",
        "operator": "not_in",
        "value": [
          "DOCUMENT_FIELDS_VALID",
          "CROSS_DOCUMENT_MATCHED",
          "OFFICIAL_PORTAL_CHECKED"
        ]
      }
    ]
  },
  "failure_code": "CSR1_MISSING_OR_UNVERIFIED",
  "failure_message": "This route requires a CSR registration credential that is not sufficiently supported."
}

17.2 Entity Comparison Process

  1. Normalize names.
  2. Compare exact identifiers.
  3. Compare dates and validity periods.
  4. Compare entity types.
  5. Compare source authority.
  6. Compare temporal context.
  7. Calculate contradiction severity.
  8. Create a review task when deterministic resolution is impossible.

17.3 Name Normalization


LEGAL_SUFFIXES = {
    "private limited",
    "pvt ltd",
    "limited",
    "foundation",
    "trust",
    "society",
    "section 8 company"
}

def normalize_entity_name(value: str) -> str:
    value = value.lower().strip()
    value = remove_punctuation(value)
    value = normalize_whitespace(value)
    return value

Legal suffix removal may be used for candidate matching, but exact legal-name comparison must retain the original value.

17.4 Contradiction Severity

  • CRITICAL: Identity, PAN or material legal conflict. Block decision.
  • HIGH: Expired mandatory credential or false live-opportunity claim. Block or review.
  • MEDIUM: Geographic, budget or date discrepancy requiring clarification.
  • LOW: Non-material textual inconsistency.

17.5 LLM-Assisted Contradiction Discovery

An LLM may propose possible contradictions by identifying semantically conflicting statements. Every proposed contradiction must be validated against structured evidence before becoming an official alert.

19.4 Prompt Requirements

Every extraction prompt must state:

  • Use only the supplied document.
  • Do not infer missing facts.
  • Return null when evidence is absent.
  • Quote the exact supporting passage.
  • Return the page number.
  • Separate historical facts from current facts.
  • Do not treat approved projects as open applications.
  • Do not treat registration as endorsement.

19.5 Prompt-Injection Defence

External documents are untrusted input.

  • Never execute instructions found inside documents.
  • Wrap document text as data, not system instructions.
  • Use fixed system prompts.
  • Disable tool access for extraction models.
  • Validate outputs against JSON Schema.
  • Reject fields without evidence spans.
  • Limit document size and token count.
  • Scan extracted text for injection patterns and record them.

  • Input document hash

21.2 Decision Policy


def derive_final_action(
    source_state,
    eligibility_status,
    contradictions,
    evidence_completeness
):
    if any(c.severity == "CRITICAL" for c in contradictions):
        return "RESOLVE_DOCUMENT_CONFLICT"

    if eligibility_status == "INELIGIBLE":
        return "DO_NOT_PURSUE"

    if source_state == "LIVE_RFP" and eligibility_status == "ELIGIBLE":
        if evidence_completeness >= 0.85:
            return "APPLY_NOW"
        return "PREPARE_AND_APPLY"

    if source_state == "BOARD_APPROVED_PROJECT":
        return "MONITOR_FOR_RFP"

    if source_state == "HISTORICAL_EXPENDITURE":
        return "CONTACT_CSR_TEAM"

    if source_state == "UNKNOWN_STATUS":
        return "INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE"

    return "HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED"

26.3 Temporal Rules


def determine_freshness(
    retrieved_at,
    last_verified_at,
    deadline,
    refresh_policy_days
):
    now = utcnow()

    if deadline and deadline < now.date():
        return "EXPIRED"

    reference = last_verified_at or retrieved_at

    if not reference:
        return "UNKNOWN"

    age_days = (now - reference).days

    if age_days <= refresh_policy_days:
        return "FRESH"

    if age_days <= refresh_policy_days * 2:
        return "AGING"

    return "STALE"

38. AI Evaluation Framework

38.1 Required Labelled Dataset

38.2 Metrics

Eligibility Accuracy

$$\text{Eligibility Accuracy} = \frac{\text{Correct eligibility decisions}}{\text{Total labelled decisions}}$$

Unsafe Approval Rate

$$\text{Unsafe Approval Rate} = \frac{\text{Ineligible routes approved}}{\text{Total ineligible routes}}$$

Source-State Accuracy

$$\text{Source-State Accuracy} = \frac{\text{Correct state classifications}}{\text{Total classified records}}$$

Citation Accuracy

$$\text{Citation Accuracy} = \frac{\text{Claims supported by the cited evidence}}{\text{Total generated claims}}$$

Contradiction Precision

$$\text{Contradiction Precision} = \frac{\text{Correct contradiction alerts}}{\text{All contradiction alerts}}$$

Rejection Explanation Coverage

$$\text{Coverage} = \frac{\text{Rejected decisions with an explicit failed rule}}{\text{Total rejected decisions}}$$

38.3 Initial Targets

  • Eligibility accuracy: at least 90%
  • Unsafe approval rate: below 5%
  • Source-state accuracy: at least 90%
  • Citation accuracy: at least 95%
  • Contradiction precision: at least 85%
  • Rejection explanation coverage: 100%

These values are targets. They must not be presented as achieved until measured.

44. Agentic Development Instructions

Any autonomous coding agent implementing this system must follow these rules:

  1. Do not change the product from decision assurance into a generic recommendation marketplace.
  2. Do not place final legal or eligibility decisions inside an LLM prompt.
  3. Do not label historical expenditure as an open opportunity.
  4. Do not claim government endorsement from registration evidence.
  5. Do not fabricate unavailable APIs.
  6. Do not bypass CAPTCHA, OTP, login or access restrictions.
  7. Do not store unencrypted sensitive identifiers.
  8. Do not produce a factual claim without an evidence record.
  9. Do not silently discard contradictory evidence.
  10. Do not delete old evidence when a source changes. Preserve versions.
  11. Do not expose one organization's data to another organization.
  12. Do not treat model confidence as evidence confidence.
  13. Do not write extracted fields directly into verified fields without validation.
  14. Every decision must store rule, source and model versions.
  15. Every rejected candidate must have an explicit rejection reason.
  16. Every uncertain case must be represented as uncertain.

46. Canonical Product Description for AI Agents


Build NIRNAY-CSR as a multi-tenant, evidence-first CSR opportunity
intelligence and decision-assurance platform.

The system must ingest permitted public CSR data, official corporate
disclosures, public RFPs and organization-supplied compliance documents.

For every candidate record, the system must independently determine:

1. What kind of record it is:
   live RFP, open channel, Board-approved project, historical expenditure,
   completed project, citizen-only scheme, private listing, expired record
   or unknown-status record.

2. Whether the current organization is eligible:
   eligible, ineligible, conditionally eligible, needs information,
   needs human review or not applicable.

3. Whether any evidence conflicts:
   identity, PAN, registration number, entity type, date, geography,
   budget, beneficiary, status or unsupported endorsement conflict.

4. What action is safe:
   apply, prepare and apply, monitor, contact the CSR team, complete a
   credential, resolve a conflict, do not pursue or request human review.

The language model may extract fields, locate evidence and explain results.
It must never make the final eligibility decision. Eligibility must be
determined using deterministic, versioned rules.

Every factual output must reference stored evidence. Every evidence record
must retain its source, URL, retrieval date, publication date, document
page, evidence text, evidence level and confidence.

Historical CSR expenditure must never be presented as an open funding
opportunity. Registration must never be presented as government
endorsement. Missing evidence must produce an unknown or review status,
not a confident positive answer.

Deploy the system using Next.js, FastAPI, PostgreSQL with pgvector, Redis,
asynchronous workers, S3-compatible object storage, Docker Compose, Nginx,
TLS, monitoring, audit logging and encrypted backups.

The system must remain operational when an external source or LLM is
temporarily unavailable. It must not bypass access controls or depend on
undocumented private APIs.

The final product is not another CSR marketplace. It is an independent,
cross-source decision-assurance layer that produces reproducible,
audit-ready evidence dockets for NGOs, corporate CSR teams and integrated
funding platforms.
